Problem Statement

There is a gap between the capacity of residents and local government officials and what is required of residents and local government officials for local government questions and processes.

Built for My World

As a former City Manager and current community developer, I encounter a lot of mistrust between people and their government. Most people meet their government at the local level, in small offices where residents aren't bureaucrats and officials aren't administrative experts. Zoning codes are long and technical, and touch on people's most basic property rights, so a simple request like splitting a lot can turn confusing and personal on both sides.

Solution

CityCenter uses Meta's models to carry that process, solve technical problems, and empower people and local government officials to close the trust gap (see the model below). Residents describe what they want in their own words and get a completed city-style application with supporting information. City officials get a four-step review with the requirements quoted from the code. The AI never recommends a decision, predicts an outcome or rates the applicant. It just puts the technical tools in people's hands to ensure every neighbor gets the same fair process.

Everything shown was built during the hackathon, Oct 26 – Nov 4, 2026. Project scope was largely limited to land use issues (i.e., the Planning and Zoning Department) for the purposes of this competition because it is where the biggest trust gap is; however, it can be expanded to all local government areas.

Questions

How many potential users are there for this program?
Potentially every person who lives in a country where local governments have the bulk of the governing and service responsibilities. In the U.S., that means more than 310 million citizens1, among about 342 million residents2, served by about 91,000 local governments3.
  1. 312.2 million citizens: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 5-year estimates, 2020–2024, as reported by The American Redistricting Project, 2030 Citizen Apportionment.
  2. 341.8 million residents on July 1, 2025: U.S. Census Bureau, Vintage 2025 national population change chart.
  3. 90,837 local governments in 2022 and 91,438 in 2025: U.S. Census Bureau, Government Organization Summary Report: 2022 & 2025.
Would local governments have to adopt this program for it to be useful to the end user?
No. Local governments' applications, codes and processes are public record, so the program could work for every citizen whether or not the local government adopts the tools for reviewing questions and applications. It would just be submitted by email instead of through the program.
What is the potential impact of this project?
In addition to helping reduce problems like housing affordability by making the land use and development process easier to navigate, there are less quantifiable benefits that are extremely impactful. By easing the administrative burden on both residents and local officials, this program builds trust and frees up time for personal connections.
What is the processing cost, and is it economical?
Yes, it is economical. At Meta's published prices, the AI work costs about 12 cents per resident request, measured across 22 test requests on November 4, 2026 (about 61,000 input tokens and 9,600 output tokens each)4. A complete application, with a few back-and-forth turns, the staff's four-step review and a second read, comes to roughly 50 cents to $1. Hosting this demo is a separate fixed cost of about $7 a month.

Compare that with what an application already costs people. Application fees commonly run from hundreds of dollars to more than a thousand: Washington City, Utah charges $400 for a minor subdivision plat and $300 plus acreage and notice fees for a zone change5, and Oakley, Utah set $750 plus a $500 retainer for a minor subdivision6. In my experience, people often spend about that much again on consultants or engineering firms just to prepare the application, separate from the actual engineering. For scale, the median U.S. planner earns about $40 an hour before any consulting markup7.

CityCenter doesn't remove the city's fee, or the need for surveying and engineering. It means the AI's share of preparing and reviewing an application costs well under a dollar.

  1. Meta Model API, Pricing and rate limits: Muse Spark at $1.25 per million input tokens and $4.25 per million output tokens; Muse Wonder at $4.00 and $20.00. Token counts are from this project's own test run. The full-application figure is an estimate built from those prices and our usage logs.
  2. Washington City, Utah, Planning fee schedule.
  3. Oakley City, Utah, Resolution 2021-9, planning and development fees.
  4. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Urban and Regional Planners: median pay of $83,720 a year, or $40.25 an hour, in May 2024.
